# M4 closes: fourteen issues, five decisions, and the pipeline reaches its report

Third entry for this date and the close of the arc the morning's entry opened. All fourteen
remaining M4 issues merged — #99 through #112 — and the milestone has no open issues. The
decision log moved from DEC-051 to DEC-056. The pipeline that could consolidate findings this
morning can now deduplicate them, survive both checkpoints, render a sixteen-section report with
a manifest, and grade itself against the ForgeFlow truth set.

## The question the morning entry left, answered before #103 needed it

DEC-046's open question — append to `downgrade_reason` or overwrite — resolved to **append,
never overwrite**, with `downgraded_from` first-writer (DEC-055). The reasoning that settled it:
the reason field answers "why is this not what the agent said", which can accumulate answers
across two nodes, while the origin field answers "what did the agent say", which has exactly one.
Overwriting would have been a silent erasure inside the field that exists to prevent silent
changes. Finding Consolidation now performs the conditions-2/3 downgrade DEC-046 assigned to it,
which had sat unimplemented between the two nodes since that entry was written.

## Two DEC-052 amendments inside one day

DEC-052 fixed `MergeDecision` at two values in the morning; DEC-054 added a third — `reviewer` —
by the afternoon, because the checkpoint work surfaced the ordinary case the two-value vocabulary
could not represent: a reviewer merging on their own judgment, prompted by no model. The lesson
worth keeping is that the two-value design was derived from the automatic path's perspective, and
the reviewer's was one issue further down the backlog. A vocabulary authored while implementing
one consumer will meet its second consumer within days here, and the cheap fix was possible only
because the first decision had stated its reasoning narrowly enough to amend rather than repeal.

## What the stale issues predated, this batch

The standing rule — issue bodies are snapshots, the decision log governs — earned itself three
times over:

- **#103's override premise is unbuildable.** The issue requires approving an `unsupported`
  finding with an override; DEC-013 and DEC-050 made such a finding unconstructible months of
  decisions ago. DEC-055 records that rather than weakening the schema to satisfy a stale
  acceptance criterion: the gate's refusal is tested against a validation-bypassing construct,
  and the override machinery exists so a future table widening is loud rather than silent.
- **#102's merge-disposition gap** resolved the way DEC-030 resolved severity: no new enum value,
  because section 18 names actions and section 4.6 names dispositions and the two do not
  correspond one to one. A reviewer merge is edits carrying the deltas plus the same
  `FindingMergeRecord` as the automatic path.
- **#109's file list was two decisions out of date** — M3 had already authored questions and
  rejections, and DEC-029 had moved FND-001 to GAP-004. What was left to author was exactly the
  outcome side: findings, gaps, observations, and the reviewer notes.

## A blocking question pauses nothing

The `Question.blocking` field was documented as "whether workflow should pause", which contradicts
DEC-005's two structural checkpoints — a field that could pause the pipeline anywhere would be a
third checkpoint configurable per question by whatever writes the field. DEC-054 corrected the
document: blocking means the assessment cannot conclude soundly without the answer, the question
leads the review package, and the reviewer decides what it holds up. Between consolidation and
checkpoint 2 no person is present, so a mid-pipeline pause would stop the process exactly where
nobody is looking at it.

## The renderer follows the template instead of reimplementing it

The report renderer parses `templates/report-v1.md` and substitutes its three marker forms, so
headings, numbering, anchors, ownership comments, and the authored empty-section wording all come
from the artifact a person edits. This fell out of DEC-035 calling the template a structural
specification: the cheapest way to keep a specification and an implementation in agreement turned
out to be making the implementation read the specification. An edit to the template changes the
report with no code change, and the template test suite holds both to the decision table.

## DEC-056 closes DEC-029's scoring question

A consolidated finding scores **full credit per matched expectation**. The argument that decided
it: the truth set records the finer decomposition *because* a matcher can collapse entries and
cannot split them — so if collapsing is an accepted mechanic, penalising the run for triggering
it makes the score depend on an authoring choice the run cannot see. The consolidation count is
recorded in the metric's notes instead, so over-merging drifts into view without wearing a
penalty's name.

## The trap that now has three tally marks

The self-scanning regression test found its own literals in its own assertion — the third
occurrence after the M3 regression suite and the consolidation quota test. The names are
assembled from parts, again. At three occurrences this is a pattern worth stating as a rule: any
test that greps its own source will match its own assertion, and the fix is always to build the
needle at runtime.
