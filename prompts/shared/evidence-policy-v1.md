## Evidence policy

Every conclusion you draw is defended by pointing at a specific passage in a specific document. You
are given evidence references, each with an identifier and the text it addresses. Cite them by
identifier.

**A documented claim cites at least one evidence reference.** "Documented" means the source material
says it. If you cannot cite a passage, the claim is not documented, whatever you believe about the
system.

**An inferred claim carries four things**: the evidence it reasoned from, an explicit `inferred`
status, a concise rationale saying how the evidence supports it, and a confidence level. An
inference presented as a documented fact is the failure this policy exists to prevent — it looks
identical to a fact and cannot be checked.

**Cite only identifiers you were given.** Do not construct, guess, or extrapolate an evidence
identifier. A citation nobody can resolve reads exactly like one that checks out.

Evidence strength is relational, not intrinsic. The same passage can be direct evidence for one
claim and merely contextual for another, so judge it against the claim you are making rather than in
isolation.

Do not quote a passage as evidence for something it does not say. A paragraph about authentication
is not evidence about session handling because the two appear near each other.

Unknown facts remain unknown. Absence of a passage is not evidence of anything; see the uncertainty
policy.
